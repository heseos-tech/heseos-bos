Our D2C Engine · Heseos BOS

One Platform Runs Every Customer From First Click to Installation

Heseos BOS is our in-house business operating system. Every enquiry, from ads, our WhatsApp bot, QR codes or partner referrals, lands in BOS as a lead and is tracked until the home is installed and paid for. That is what lets us open a new city with D2C in weeks instead of building a dealer network first.

Customer experience

One Owner, No Dropped Leads

Customers get a call, a demo and a quote without chasing anyone. Every step and every handover is visible in one place.

Asset-light scale

Partners and Installers on Tap

Shops, electricians, designers and builders log leads in the partner app and earn commission. Verified installers claim jobs and are paid per installation, so there is no fixed install crew to hire city by city.

Data-driven growth

Cost per Lead and Conversion, Live

Because every lead carries its source, we see what each channel costs and converts by city, and move ad budget to what works.

Risks and How We Manage Them

We Have Planned for What Could Go Wrong

Risk
How we manage it
Seed runway is tight before Series A
Series A conversations start by Month 12. If sales run 30% below plan, cash reaches about zero at Month 18, so we would trim ad spend in Months 13–18 or close Series A a month early. BOS shows the shortfall months ahead.
Lead costs rise as we add cities
The plan assumes ₹410–520 per lead and 9–12% lead-to-order conversion, with each city taking six months to ramp. BOS tracks cost and conversion by source and city every week, and partner referrals grow from 25% to 35% of D2C orders, which lowers paid lead needs.
Installation quality at scale
Installers are verified before they can claim jobs in BOS, every job is tracked to completion, and payouts are released only when the job is marked done.
Inventory and receivables absorb cash
Working capital is fully modelled: 45–60 days of stock, D2C customers pay an advance with the balance at installation, dealer credit is 15–20 days and institutional credit 60 days.
Price pressure from large brands
Own hardware design and the planned assembly line cut cost. Software, service and retrofit-first products set us apart from commodity switches.
Component cost or supply shocks
The downside case adds 3 points to hardware cost and cuts volume 30%. EBITDA still turns positive by Month 41 and the company ends Year 5 with ₹38 Cr cash.
